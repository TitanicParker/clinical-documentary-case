# Clinical Tension Register

## TENSION-0001 — Structural foot pathology vs neurological foot dysfunction

**Documentary tension:** The record contains genuine structural foot pathology — mallet/clawed toes, callus/corns, pronating foot type, focal fifth-metatarsophalangeal lesions and repeated podiatric offloading — while the neurological record also documents Parkinsonian gait/foot phenomena, medication-linked changes in the feet, a parkinsonian foot throw with normal power, OFF-related interpretations of pain and shuffling, and later toe curling. The documents therefore do not support a simple exclusively structural or exclusively neurological account.

**Anchored facts:** FACT-0001, FACT-0003, FACT-0013, FACT-0014, FACT-0024, FACT-0025, FACT-0028, FACT-0029, FACT-0030, FACT-0032, FACT-0033, FACT-0035, FACT-0036, FACT-0038

**Expert issue:** How should the structural findings and the neurological/treatment-state findings be integrated clinically, and what mechanisms could plausibly connect rigidity, dystonia, abnormal gait or altered loading to recurrent focal pressure lesions and pain?

## TENSION-0002 — Early separation vs later evidence requiring reassessment

**Documentary tension:** The feet were initially managed through a separate podiatric pathway, and the patient himself at times regarded the corns, calluses and pain as distinct from the predominant leg tension. Later evidence repeatedly crossed that boundary: the 2018 withdrawal history linked rapid recurrence of the outside-left-foot phenomenon to stopping procyclidine; the 2020 foot throw was expressly classified as parkinsonian; the 2023 nurse assessed leg pain and shuffling as OFF-related; and the 2023 consultant recorded an explicit "misalignment" over whether much of the presentation was explainable by Parkinson's disease or arose separately from the feet.

**Anchored facts:** FACT-0003, FACT-0006, FACT-0033, FACT-0035, FACT-0036, FACT-0037

**Expert issue:** At what point, if any, did the accumulated evidence make explicit reassessment of the original structural/neurological partition clinically necessary?

## TENSION-0003 — Treatment response as symptom control vs treatment response as diagnostic information

**Documentary tension:** Procyclidine produced profound global functional improvement in 2017, and withdrawal was followed by worsening tension, restless feet and gait deterioration. The 2018 neurologist then documented that the first rapidly changing feature during withdrawal was the return of the outside-left-foot phenomenon. This is stronger than a generic later recollection, but it still does not by itself identify the mechanism or prove that levodopa would have produced the same effect.

**Anchored facts:** FACT-0011, FACT-0013, FACT-0014, FACT-0015, FACT-0033

**Expert issue:** What diagnostic weight should reasonably have been attached to this withdrawal/rechallenge pattern, particularly in relation to symptom attribution and the need to revisit the relationship between the feet and the movement disorder?

## TENSION-0004 — Levodopa deferral for long-term strategy vs acknowledged contemporaneous benefit

**Documentary tension:** From 2017 onward the record shows a deliberate strategy to defer levodopa because of very young onset and concern about dyskinesia and behavioural complications. The same record acknowledged that the patient might do very well with levodopa, that clinicians might later regret not starting it earlier, and in 2018 that he "could be very much better" if levodopa or another dopamine agonist were given. Levodopa was not introduced until 2021 after the patient reported feeling undertreated.

**Anchored facts:** FACT-0009, FACT-0010, FACT-0012, FACT-0018, FACT-0019, FACT-0034, FACT-0039

**Expert issue:** Was the duration and degree of levodopa deferral clinically proportionate to the documented risks, symptoms and functional burden at each stage, rather than merely reasonable in principle at the outset?

## TENSION-0005 — Dyskinesia risk rationale vs later delayed-start explanation

**Documentary tension:** The clinical strategy repeatedly relied on concern that dopaminergic treatment in a very young patient would precipitate troublesome dyskinesia. Actual chorea/dyskinesia later occurred with dopamine agonist and levodopa exposure, supporting genuine sensitivity. In 2022, however, the neurologist recorded that delayed-start evidence indicated Sinemet may fuel or reveal dyskinesia but does not cause it, attributing the underlying cause to impaired cerebral dopamine handling.

**Anchored facts:** FACT-0012, FACT-0016, FACT-0017, FACT-0018, FACT-0021, FACT-0022, FACT-0023

**Expert issue:** Did delaying levodopa materially protect long-term outcome, delay only the expression of treatment-related dyskinesia, or mainly postpone symptomatic benefit? How should the later explanation be reconciled with the earlier rationale?

## TENSION-0006 — Genetic suspicion and stated management irrelevance vs possible treatment relevance

**Documentary tension:** In 2017 Prof Healy considered PARK2 sufficiently likely to recommend targeted testing and linked PARK2 with prolonged management on relatively low doses of dopamine replacement and dyskinesia if over-used. In 2018 the treating neurologist said genetic testing did not particularly change current management and preferred delay, while also acknowledging possible relevance to prognostication and levodopa sensitivities and expressly accepting that there was reasonable logic to testing PARK2/PRKN or the panel immediately.

**Anchored facts:** FACT-0010, FACT-0011, FACT-0012, FACT-0040

**Expert issue:** Was genetic confirmation in fact capable of informing treatment strategy, prognosis, counselling or monitoring in this very-young-onset case, and if so was deferral clinically consequential?

## TENSION-0007 — Demonstrated dopaminergic sensitivity vs documented undertreatment

**Documentary tension:** The record documents genuine difficulty balancing benefit against adverse dopaminergic effects, including compulsive-behaviour concerns and chorea/dyskinesia. It also records that the patient felt undertreated before levodopa was introduced, that by 2023 the consultant suspected much evening walking difficulty reflected lack of levodopa, and that a Parkinson's nurse expressly assessed the patient as undertreated with OFF-related leg pain and shuffling.

**Anchored facts:** FACT-0016, FACT-0017, FACT-0019, FACT-0021, FACT-0022, FACT-0024, FACT-0036

**Expert issue:** Did the treatment strategy maintain an appropriate balance between avoiding adverse effects and preventing clinically significant OFF symptoms, pain and loss of function? If the balance became inappropriate, when did that occur?

## TENSION-0008 — Medication-linked pain relief vs non-specific analgesic interpretation

**Documentary tension:** The record repeatedly links medication state to pain or foot relief, including OFF-related worsening and relief once Sinemet took effect. Yet in 2025 the neurologist expressly cautioned that Sinemet can lessen the experience of unrelated pain and stated that the described pain was not, in his view, direct Parkinson's phenomenology apart from the possibility of painful dystonia.

**Anchored facts:** FACT-0024, FACT-0028, FACT-0036, FACT-0038

**Expert issue:** What evidential weight should be given to dopaminergic responsiveness of pain, and how should non-specific pain modulation be distinguished from painful dystonia, OFF-related pain, abnormal loading or secondary musculoskeletal pain?

## TENSION-0009 — Disease progression neutrality vs symptomatic cost of delayed treatment

**Documentary tension:** The 2017 neurologist expressly stated that delaying symptomatic treatment would not disadvantage the patient from a neuropathological or disease-progression perspective. That proposition is distinct from the question whether delayed treatment imposed avoidable symptomatic or functional burden during the years of deferral.

**Anchored facts:** FACT-0009, FACT-0034, FACT-0039, FACT-0019, FACT-0036

**Expert issue:** Even if delayed levodopa did not worsen neurodegeneration, what symptomatic, functional or quality-of-life consequences, if any, would a reasonable clinician have expected from continued deferral?

## TENSION-0010 — Retrospective adverse effects vs contemporaneous decision-making

**Documentary tension:** Later dyskinesia and compulsive-behaviour concerns are real evidence of treatment sensitivity, but they occurred after the earlier decisions to defer levodopa. They may validate the original risk assessment, but they cannot automatically establish that every earlier period of restraint was proportionate.

**Anchored facts:** FACT-0016, FACT-0017, FACT-0021, FACT-0022, FACT-0034

**Expert issue:** To what extent can later adverse effects legitimately be used to evaluate the reasonableness of earlier treatment decisions, and what contemporaneous information should instead control that assessment?
