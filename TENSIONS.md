# Clinical Documentary Case — Tensions



**Status:** Analytical bridge between the controlled fact register and later clinical propositions.



**Source basis:** `FACTS.md`



> **Control rule:** A tension identifies a documentary relationship requiring explanation or expert adjudication. It is not itself a finding of causation, negligence, breach, motive or clinical error.

## TENSION-0001 — Integrated neurological presentation vs later operational separation



**Facts engaged:** FACT-0001, FACT-0002, FACT-0003, FACT-0042, FACT-0043, FACT-0044, FACT-0045, FACT-0046, FACT-0049, FACT-0050, FACT-0052



**Documentary tension:**  

The pre-admission GP record progressively brought feet, gait, impaired movement initiation, increased tone, toe clawing, dexterity and functional impairment into one neurological referral pathway. The July 2017 neurological assessment then documented both severe Parkinsonism and genuine structural foot pathology, but did not resolve their relationship. Despite that unresolved state, the foot problem was operationally routed to Podiatry while Parkinsonism remained within Neurology.



**Why it matters:**  

Parallel specialty care may have been appropriate, but operational separation is not the same thing as a clinical finding that the problems were unrelated.



**Boundary:**  

This tension does not imply that Podiatry referral was inappropriate.

## TENSION-0002 — Express “Not clear” relationship vs durable symptom partition



**Facts engaged:** FACT-0047, FACT-0048, FACT-0050, FACT-0051, FACT-0052



**Documentary tension:**  

The neurologist expressly recorded that the relationship between the leg symptoms, corns, callouses and burning was “Not clear,” while also recording that the patient himself had come to regard foot pain and leg tension as separate problems. A durable practical distinction nevertheless followed.



**Why it matters:**  

The original partition began from uncertainty rather than from a documented etiological conclusion.



**Boundary:**  

The patient's own distinction between symptom groups was clinically relevant and must not be erased.

## TENSION-0003 — Genuine structural foot disease vs neurological/dystonic lower-limb phenotype



**Facts engaged:** FACT-0001, FACT-0029, FACT-0030, FACT-0032, FACT-0049, FACT-0060, FACT-0063, FACT-0067, FACT-0075, FACT-0084, FACT-0085, FACT-0086, FACT-0087, FACT-0088, FACT-0089, FACT-0090



**Documentary tension:**  

The record contains persistent and repeatedly treated local structural pathology, especially at the left fifth MTPJ, together with toe deformity, callus/corn formation and benefit from local mechanical measures. At the same time, neurological records describe dystonic-looking gait, clawing, abnormal tone and later explicitly Parkinsonian lower-limb phenomena.



**Why it matters:**  

Neither an exclusively structural nor an exclusively neurological model adequately captures the full documentary phenotype.



**Boundary:**  

Coexistence does not itself establish the mechanism producing any individual pain episode or lesion.

## TENSION-0004 — Neurological treatment response vs persistence of the earlier separation



**Facts engaged:** FACT-0011, FACT-0013, FACT-0014, FACT-0015, FACT-0033, FACT-0056, FACT-0060, FACT-0061



**Documentary tension:**  

Low-dose neurological treatment produced marked functional improvement, and withdrawal was associated with worsening gait, tension, restless feet and the rapid return of the outside-left-foot phenomenon. These observations occurred after the original foot/neurology relationship had been left unresolved.



**Why it matters:**  

The treatment-state evidence was capable of bearing on the adequacy of the original symptom partition and therefore raises a reassessment question.



**Boundary:**  

Response to procyclidine or rasagiline does not prove that structural lesions were neurologically caused, nor does it prove what levodopa would have achieved at the same date.

## TENSION-0005 — Acknowledged potential for substantial dopaminergic benefit vs deliberate levodopa deferral



**Facts engaged:** FACT-0009, FACT-0010, FACT-0011, FACT-0012, FACT-0034, FACT-0039, FACT-0053, FACT-0054, FACT-0059, FACT-0098, FACT-0099



**Documentary tension:**  

The treating team deliberately deferred levodopa and dopamine agonists because of the patient's very young age and concern about dyskinesia, impulse-control problems and other long-term treatment complications. At the same time, the record repeatedly acknowledged that dopaminergic therapy could produce substantial benefit, that levodopa would probably eventually be required, and that there might later be regret that it had not been started sooner.



**Why it matters:**  

The clinical issue is not whether caution was legitimate, but whether the duration and degree of deferral remained proportionate as disability and treatment-state evidence accumulated.



**Boundary:**  

This tension does not establish that levodopa should have been commenced in 2017.

## TENSION-0006 — Disease-progression rationale vs symptomatic-burden rationale



**Facts engaged:** FACT-0009, FACT-0039, FACT-0053, FACT-0054, FACT-0059, FACT-0098



**Documentary tension:**  

The early record expressly asserted that delaying symptomatic dopaminergic treatment would not disadvantage the underlying neuropathological progression. That proposition is distinct from the separate question of whether delaying treatment allowed avoidable present-day disability, pain, gait impairment or functional loss.



**Why it matters:**  

A justification concerning disease progression cannot by itself resolve the proportionality of untreated or undertreated symptoms.



**Boundary:**  

The documentary record alone does not determine the counterfactual benefit or harm of earlier levodopa.

## TENSION-0007 — Dyskinesia-risk rationale vs later explanation of dyskinesia susceptibility



**Facts engaged:** FACT-0016, FACT-0017, FACT-0018, FACT-0021, FACT-0022, FACT-0065, FACT-0066, FACT-0077, FACT-0078, FACT-0081, FACT-0096



**Documentary tension:**  

Later treatment repeatedly demonstrated genuine susceptibility to chorea/dyskinesia and therefore supports the reality of the original caution. The later neurological explanation, however, distinguished levodopa as something that can fuel or expose dyskinesia rather than being the underlying cause of the susceptibility.



**Why it matters:**  

Later dyskinesia supports caution but does not automatically establish that every earlier period of levodopa deferral was proportionate.



**Boundary:**  

Later adverse effects cannot be used retrospectively as simple proof of what the correct dose or start date would have been years earlier.

## TENSION-0008 — PARK2/PRKN suspicion and management relevance vs delayed genetic resolution



**Facts engaged:** FACT-0011, FACT-0012, FACT-0040, FACT-0057, FACT-0058, FACT-0092



**Documentary tension:**  

PARK2/PRKN was a serious early diagnostic branch. Specialist correspondence recommended targeted testing, while the treating neurologist also recorded that genetic confirmation might not materially change immediate management. In 2026, genetic testing ultimately confirmed two pathogenic Parkin variants and attributed the Parkinsonism and dystonia to compound heterozygous Parkin mutations.



**Why it matters:**  

The genetic endpoint validates the earlier PRKN branch as clinically real and creates an expert question about whether earlier confirmation would have altered treatment, phenotyping, counselling or reassessment.



**Boundary:**  

The 2026 result does not retrospectively prove the mechanism of every historical foot lesion or pain episode.

**Documentary tension:**  

By 2020, the neurologist expressly characterised the foot-throw phenomenon as Parkinsonian despite normal power. Later records considered OFF dystonia, toe curling, dyskinetic gait and medication-state effects while structural foot pain and corn/callus pathology continued.



**Why it matters:**

## TENSION-0010 — Explicit 2023 undertreatment/OFF interpretation vs the preceding treatment strategy

**Documentary tension:**  

In 2023, the consultant recorded a reasonable suspicion that substantial walking difficulty reflected lack of levodopa, while the Parkinson's nurse expressly recorded that the patient appeared undertreated and that leg pain and shuffling were OFF-related. This followed years of deliberate restraint in dopaminergic escalation.



**Why it matters:**  

The 2023 records create a strong documentary endpoint at which undertreatment was no longer merely a retrospective allegation but was expressly recorded within the treating service.



**Boundary:**

## TENSION-0011 — Patient-clinician “misalignment” vs convergence of later evidence



**Facts engaged:** FACT-0025, FACT-0037, FACT-0071, FACT-0072, FACT-0073, FACT-0093, FACT-0097



**Documentary tension:**  

The patient continued to regard the feet as a major separate problem, while the neurologist increasingly interpreted gait, dystonia and OFF-state phenomena through Parkinsonism. The neurologist later expressly described a “misalignment” while acknowledging the patient's concerns as reasonable and justifiable.



**Why it matters:**  

The record shows not simply disagreement, but competing explanatory models operating over the same evolving symptom complex.



**Boundary:**  

Neither the patient's model nor the clinician's model is converted into an objective fact merely because it was repeatedly expressed.

## TENSION-0012 — Medication-responsive pain vs non-specific pain modulation



**Facts engaged:** FACT-0024, FACT-0028, FACT-0038, FACT-0062, FACT-0079, FACT-0080, FACT-0081, FACT-0094, FACT-0095, FACT-0100



**Documentary tension:**  

Pain repeatedly improved when Parkinson's medication was effective, and later Neurology expressly accepted that Sinemet helped the patient's pain. The same neurologist also stated that levodopa can reduce the experience of pain arising from other causes and that the described pain was not necessarily direct Parkinsonian phenomenology.



**Why it matters:**  

Medication response is clinically important evidence of interaction with treatment state, but it is not mechanistically specific.



**Boundary:**  

Sinemet-related pain relief does not by itself establish whether the underlying generator was dystonic, neuropathic, mechanical, structural or mixed.

## TENSION-0013 — Pregabalin response and local treatment benefit vs dopaminergic interaction

**Documentary tension:**

**Why it matters:**

## TENSION-0014 — Cautious Sinemet escalation vs profound Sinemet CR benefit



**Facts engaged:** FACT-0081, FACT-0082, FACT-0083, FACT-0096, FACT-0100, FACT-0101, FACT-0102, FACT-0103



**Documentary tension:**  

The 2025 neurologist deliberately limited dopaminergic escalation because greater Sinemet exposure was expected to worsen dyskinesia and instead proposed cautious nocturnal controlled-release treatment. The subsequent clinician response recorded “profound relief currently,” and the Quality and Patient Safety letter described Sinemet CR as an effective analgesic for the foot pain.



**Why it matters:**  

The later response strengthens the counterfactual expert question about whether the same therapeutic possibility should reasonably have been explored earlier, while leaving the adverse-effect trade-off intact.



**Boundary:**  

A successful 2025 trial does not prove that the same treatment would have produced the same net benefit at an earlier date.

## TENSION-0015 — 2026 Parkin confirmation vs historical interpretation of dystonia and foot phenomena



**Facts engaged:** FACT-0049, FACT-0050, FACT-0057, FACT-0058, FACT-0092



**Documentary tension:**  

The 2017 record described dystonic-looking lower-limb posture while leaving the relationship between the neurological and foot domains unresolved. In 2026 a movement-disorders specialist attributed both Parkinsonism and dystonia to genetically confirmed compound heterozygous Parkin mutations.



**Why it matters:**  

The later result materially strengthens the legitimacy of asking whether historical dystonic lower-limb phenomena were adequately integrated into the evolving clinical model.



**Boundary:**  

The genetic result establishes the cause of the Parkinsonism and dystonia as recorded by the specialist; it does not identify every historical pain or structural lesion as dystonic.

## TENSION-0016 — Defensible initial uncertainty vs threshold for mandatory reassessment



**Facts engaged:** FACT-0049, FACT-0050, FACT-0052, FACT-0033, FACT-0034, FACT-0035, FACT-0023, FACT-0024, FACT-0036, FACT-0072, FACT-0079, FACT-0101, FACT-0092



**Documentary tension:**  

The initial 2017 mixed presentation could reasonably support uncertainty and parallel structural and neurological management. Over time, however, procyclidine withdrawal phenomena, acknowledged potential dopaminergic benefit, explicitly Parkinsonian foot signs, first levodopa treatment, later OFF-state interpretations, explicit undertreatment, medication-linked pain relief, profound Sinemet CR response and eventual Parkin confirmation accumulated.



**Why it matters:**  

The central longitudinal question is therefore not whether the original 2017 strategy was necessarily wrong. It is **when, if ever, the accumulated evidence crossed a threshold at which the original explanatory partition or treatment strategy required explicit reassessment.**



**Boundary:**  

The date and clinical significance of any such threshold require independent expert judgment.

## TENSION-0017 — Early PRKN suspicion vs 2025 idiopathic framing vs 2026 genetic confirmation

**Facts engaged:** FACT-0057, FACT-0058, FACT-0092, FACT-0093

**Documentary tension:**  
In 2017, Neurology regarded autosomal-recessive Parkinsonism as plausible and specifically identified PRKN/PARK2 as a serious diagnostic possibility. In March 2025, before genetic confirmation, the treating neurologist nevertheless described the current belief and understanding as “idiopathic Parkinson’s disease.” In February 2026, specialist genetic testing confirmed two pathogenic Parkin variants and concluded that the patient’s Parkinsonism and dystonia were due to compound heterozygous Parkin mutations.

**Why it matters:**  
The 2026 result does not make the 2025 description unreasonable merely because it was later superseded. It does, however, confirm that the early PRKN branch represented the eventual documented aetiology and raises a legitimate expert question about whether earlier genetic clarification could have affected phenotyping, interpretation of dystonia, treatment expectations or longitudinal reassessment.

**Boundary:**  
The genetic result does not establish that every historical foot symptom or pain episode was caused by Parkin-related dystonia.

# Governing synthesis



The documentary record supports two propositions simultaneously:



1. **There was genuine structural and mechanical foot pathology requiring local treatment.**

2. **The lower-limb and foot phenotype also repeatedly interacted with dystonia, Parkinsonian motor state and dopaminergic treatment.**



The strongest tension is therefore not structural **versus** neurological causation in absolute terms.



It is whether an initially unresolved and plausibly mixed presentation became too durably partitioned, and whether later accumulating evidence required a documented reassessment of that partition and of the associated dopaminergic treatment strategy.
