# Clinical Fact Register

## FACT-0001

**Fact:** On 10 April 2017, the GP recorded bilateral foot pain and abnormal gait. The same entry recorded marked mallet toes, onychogryphosis and callouses/pressure areas, and planned referral to Podiatry.

**Source:** CLIN-000018–CLIN-000028

## FACT-0002

**Fact:** By 29 June 2017, worsening dexterity, abnormal gait and increased tone were affecting work and daily activities, and the GP sought urgent medical assessment because the condition appeared to be deteriorating.

**Source:** CLIN-000866–CLIN-000884

## FACT-0003

**Fact:** On 6 July 2017, the GP telephoned Podiatry and recorded that the case would be marked as a priority and "as Parkinson's."

**Source:** CLIN-000236–CLIN-000245

## FACT-0004

**Fact:** The July 2017 hospital discharge record identified Parkinson's disease as the primary diagnosis.

**Source:** CLIN-000263–CLIN-000265

## FACT-0005

**Fact:** The July 2017 discharge record described difficulty initiating movement, bradykinesia, reduced dexterity, impaired balance and an unsteady gait of two to three years' duration.

**Source:** CLIN-000279–CLIN-000283

## FACT-0006

**Fact:** During the July 2017 admission, clonazepam was used for leg tension and a Podiatry referral was sent for the reported foot problems.

**Source:** CLIN-000341–CLIN-000345

## FACT-0007

**Fact:** On discharge, the neurological regimen included rasagiline and procyclidine, alongside clonazepam and other listed medications.

**Source:** CLIN-000354–CLIN-000365

## FACT-0008

**Fact:** The 13 July 2017 DaTSCAN was abnormal, with marked bilateral basal-ganglia uptake truncation reported as consistent with idiopathic Parkinson's disease or Lewy body dementia; the treating neurologist recorded that the presentation was not Lewy body dementia.

**Source:** CLIN-001851–CLIN-001871

## FACT-0009

**Fact:** The 2017 neurology record stated that the patient understood the decision to delay levodopa or dopamine-agonist treatment, while also recording that such treatment might later be needed and that clinicians might in time regret not starting levodopa earlier.

**Source:** CLIN-001601–CLIN-001607

## FACT-0010

**Fact:** In 2017, Dr Thomas Monaghan sought Prof Dan Healy's advice on genetic testing and a possible second opinion and wrote that "Myself and Tim" were keen to hold back on levodopa insofar as possible unless Healy advised otherwise.

**Source:** CLIN-001724–CLIN-001730

## FACT-0011

**Fact:** On 12 September 2017, Prof Daniel Healy recorded that the patient probably had "Park II," recommended testing that initially and then a panel if negative, and recorded dramatic motor improvement with low-dose procyclidine and rasagiline.

**Source:** CLIN-001741–CLIN-001746

## FACT-0012

**Fact:** Prof Healy further recorded that patients with PARK II can manage for long periods on relatively low doses of dopamine-replacement therapy but can become dyskinetic if over-used.

**Source:** CLIN-001747–CLIN-001749

## FACT-0013

**Fact:** In a contemporaneous 4 October 2017 email, the patient reported marked functional improvement during procyclidine treatment, including restoration of walking, writing, standing upright, leg looseness and several ordinary movement and dexterity tasks.

**Source:** CLIN-001770–CLIN-001781

## FACT-0014

**Fact:** Four days into the planned procyclidine withdrawal, the patient contemporaneously reported increasing tension, restless feet and a deteriorating walk, while his eyesight returned to normal.

**Source:** CLIN-001782–CLIN-001790

## FACT-0015

**Fact:** A 24 October 2017 email to workplace HR recorded that medication had been stopped because of visual problems, restarted at a lower dose, and then increased again because the lower dose did not cover all symptoms; the patient simultaneously reported profound physical improvement.

**Source:** CLIN-001792–CLIN-001801

## FACT-0016

**Fact:** In 2019, the neurology record documented chorea/dyskinesia on higher Neupro doses together with troublesome compulsive-behaviour concerns, while a lower dose was regarded as more stable.

**Source:** CLIN-002129–CLIN-002138

## FACT-0017

**Fact:** In 2019, the neurologist described dopamine treatment as a "tight rope" between stiffness and lack of movement on one side and impulsivity, compulsive behaviours, obsessionality and chorea/dyskinesia on the other.

**Source:** CLIN-002152–CLIN-002156

## FACT-0018

**Fact:** The same 2019 record stated that levodopa would eventually have to be considered but that, because of the patient's young age, the neurologist preferred to leave it for as long as humanly possible.

**Source:** CLIN-002157–CLIN-002159

## FACT-0019

**Fact:** In September 2021, after the patient felt undertreated, Sinemet 62.5 mg twice daily was introduced and was described as the first start of a levodopa drug.

**Source:** CLIN-002334–CLIN-002336

## FACT-0020

**Fact:** By October 2021, Kemadrin was being removed because of raised ocular pressure and Sinemet was being increased to four times daily to compensate.

**Source:** CLIN-002336–CLIN-002338

## FACT-0021

**Fact:** In October 2021, the neurologist expressed concern about allowing Sinemet to rise significantly above the then-current dose because slight chorea had already appeared.

**Source:** CLIN-002347–CLIN-002352

## FACT-0022

**Fact:** In 2022, the neurologist recorded that even minimal levodopa exposure could produce mild excess movement and that a higher Stalevo dose had previously produced obvious dyskinesia/chorea.

**Source:** CLIN-002395–CLIN-002400

## FACT-0023

**Fact:** The 2022 record expressly stated that delayed-start studies showed Sinemet may fuel or power dyskinesia but does not cause it, attributing the cause to impaired cerebral handling of normal dopamine.

**Source:** CLIN-002398–CLIN-002400

## FACT-0024

**Fact:** In 2023, the neurologist recorded a reasonable suspicion that much of the evening inability to walk reflected lack of levodopa and noted that Stalevo facilitated walking and darts while also being experienced by the patient as helping pain.

**Source:** CLIN-002467–CLIN-002474

## FACT-0025

**Fact:** The 2023 neurology record documented an explicit "misalignment": the neurologist considered much of the presentation explainable by Parkinson's disease, while the patient considered a separate foot problem to be a major part of what was occurring.

**Source:** CLIN-002475–CLIN-002478

## FACT-0026

**Fact:** A March 2023 neurology-team email recorded debilitating bilateral foot/leg pain despite pregabalin and the patient's view at that time that the pain was not Parkinson-related.

**Source:** CLIN-002675–CLIN-002684

## FACT-0027

**Fact:** In 2024, the patient was recorded as functioning within narrower limits, with evening fatigue and toe curling during activity, while the neurologist continued to balance evening benefit from Sinemet against the risk of dyskinesia.

**Source:** CLIN-002695–CLIN-002710

## FACT-0028

**Fact:** In 2025, the patient described longstanding predominantly left-sided foot pain, severe pain during the first steps of the morning, and relief in the feet once Parkinson's medication took effect.

**Source:** CLIN-002777–CLIN-002787

## FACT-0029

**Fact:** The 15 June 2018 Podiatry record documented callus at the left fifth metatarsophalangeal joint and clawing of toes 1–3 on both feet.

**Source:** CLIN-002943–CLIN-002955

## FACT-0030

**Fact:** On 7 December 2018, Podiatry recorded pain under the left side of the left foot, callus at the left fifth metatarsophalangeal joint, a pronating foot type and a slight leg-length discrepancy, and provided renewed offloading/orthotic treatment.

**Source:** CLIN-002958–CLIN-002971

## FACT-0031

**Fact:** The original Podiatry referral was received on 10 April 2017 and recorded corn/callus, thickened nails, forefoot/rearfoot or arch pain, toe deformities and gait abnormalities; the later "PARKINSONS" annotation cannot safely be assumed to have been contemporaneous with the original referral.

**Source:** CLIN-002989–CLIN-003012

## FACT-0032

**Fact:** A 2019 Podiatry re-referral expressly described prior foot problems as relating to increased tone and abnormal gait associated with Parkinson's disease and recorded current callus, gait abnormality, "clawing feet" and pain.

**Source:** CLIN-003090–CLIN-003106

## FACT-0033

**Fact:** The 2018 neurology record documented the earlier two-week procyclidine withdrawal and stated that the first thing to change rapidly was the return of the outside-left-foot corn phenomenon; the patient also became more "shifty" and "unrelaxed" off procyclidine.

**Source:** CLIN-001914–CLIN-001918

## FACT-0034

**Fact:** In 2018, the neurologist recorded that the patient "could be very much better" if given levodopa or another dopamine agonist, while stating that for clearly set-out reasons the team did not want to do so for quite some time.

**Source:** CLIN-001939–CLIN-001945

## FACT-0035

**Fact:** In 2020, the neurologist recorded a left lower-limb "foot throw" that resembled foot drop but classified it as parkinsonian because dorsiflexion, plantarflexion, inversion and eversion power were normal; the same passage recorded exquisite foot sensitivity, excessive movement and an urge to move the feet.

**Source:** CLIN-002284–CLIN-002289

## FACT-0036

**Fact:** In 2023, a Parkinson's nurse recorded severe foot pain, reduced mobility and shuffling that worsened in the morning and later in the day if Sinemet had not been taken, assessed that the patient appeared undertreated and that the leg pain and shuffling were OFF-related, and documented commencement of Stalevo after discussion with the consultant neurologist.

**Source:** CLIN-002573–CLIN-002581

## FACT-0037

**Fact:** In 2023, the treating neurologist recorded the patient's reasonable and justifiable concern that much of the problem related to the feet separately, noted the patient's account that the very first symptoms had been in the feet, and also recorded that peripheral neuropathic symptoms are very common in Parkinson's disease while arranging Fabry testing as a possible overlapping explanation.

**Source:** CLIN-002475–CLIN-002481

## FACT-0038

**Fact:** In 2025, the neurologist recorded that Sinemet-related relief of foot pain did not by itself establish a Parkinsonian pain generator, explaining that Sinemet can lessen the experience of unrelated pain; the same note stated that painful dystonia can occur in Parkinson's disease but that the pain then being described was not viewed as direct Parkinson's phenomenology.

**Source:** CLIN-002787–CLIN-002789

## FACT-0039

**Fact:** The 2017 neurology record expressly stated that delaying symptomatic treatment would not disadvantage the patient from a neuropathological or disease-progression perspective.

**Source:** CLIN-001601–CLIN-001612

## FACT-0040

**Fact:** In 2018, the neurologist stated that genetic testing did not particularly change current management and therefore saw no great loss in not knowing immediately, while also recording that there was reasonable logic to performing isolated PARK2/PRKN testing or the 30-gene panel at that time and that Prof Healy would support that approach.

**Source:** CLIN-001955–CLIN-001956
