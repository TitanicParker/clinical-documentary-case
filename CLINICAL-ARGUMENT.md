# Documentary Clinical Argument

## 1. Central issue

The documentary case is not that the patient had either Parkinsonism or a separate foot disorder. The record supports both neurological disease and genuine structural foot pathology. The central issue is whether the evolving relationship between those two streams was recognised, reassessed and managed coherently over time.

The strongest documentary question is therefore one of integration: whether recurrent focal foot pathology, abnormal gait, dystonic or Parkinsonian foot phenomena, pain, medication response and later OFF-state interpretations were treated as parts of a single evolving clinical problem where appropriate, or whether an early division between neurological and structural symptoms remained operational after the evidence supporting that division had weakened.

## 2. The starting position in 2017 was clinically mixed

The early record already contained two categories of evidence.

On the structural side, the patient had bilateral foot pain, mallet or clawed toes, callus and pressure lesions, nail pathology and later repeated podiatric findings requiring offloading and orthotic management.

On the neurological side, the same period documented difficulty initiating movement, increased tone, bradykinesia, impaired dexterity, abnormal gait and a subsequent diagnosis of very-young-onset Parkinsonism supported by an abnormal DaTSCAN.

That combination made parallel neurological and podiatric management reasonable at the outset. The documentary case does not require the proposition that all foot symptoms were neurological from the beginning.

**Relevant propositions:** PROP-0001, PROP-0002

## 3. Treatment response introduced evidence that the streams were not cleanly separable

The 2017 response to procyclidine and rasagiline was striking. The patient contemporaneously described restoration of walking, writing, upright posture, leg looseness and broader movement. During planned procyclidine withdrawal he reported increasing tension, restless feet and deterioration of gait.

The significance of those observations is not that they prove the foot pathology was Parkinsonian. Procyclidine could have altered tone, movement, loading, pain perception or several mechanisms at once. Their importance is narrower and stronger: the disputed lower-limb and foot presentation was demonstrably modifiable by neurological treatment.

That evidence made the relationship between the neurological disorder and the foot presentation a live clinical question rather than a settled separation.

**Relevant propositions:** PROP-0003, PROP-0004

## 4. The foot record remained structurally real

Podiatry continued to document callus, clawing, focal fifth-metatarsophalangeal pathology, pronation and recurrent need for mechanical offloading. Those findings cannot simply be reclassified as neurological symptoms.

But structural findings answer a different question from causation. A corn or callus establishes a local lesion; it does not by itself establish why abnormal pressure repeatedly arose. In a patient with rigidity, abnormal tone, dystonia and altered gait, a structural lesion could be independent, could be aggravated by neurological loading, or could arise partly downstream from it.

The documentary record therefore supports a mixed-mechanism hypothesis that requires expert adjudication.

**Relevant propositions:** PROP-0001, PROP-0002, PROP-0011

## 5. The original separation became progressively harder to maintain without explicit reassessment

Later records add several pieces of evidence.

The neurological record came to describe foot and gait phenomena as Parkinsonian. The treatment state increasingly affected walking and pain. By 2023 the treating neurologist recorded a "misalignment": the patient regarded much of the problem as separate foot pathology, while the clinician considered much of it explainable by Parkinson's disease.

By 2025 the record documented relief in the feet once Parkinson's medication took effect.

None of these entries retrospectively proves a single neurological generator. Taken longitudinally, however, they make the adequacy of the earlier partition increasingly dependent on explicit reassessment. The documentary question becomes whether the clinical model evolved as the evidence evolved.

**Relevant propositions:** PROP-0004, PROP-0011

## 6. Levodopa was consciously deferred

The record is clear that levodopa was not overlooked. It was deliberately postponed because of the patient's exceptional youth and concern about dyskinesia and behavioural complications.

The strategy was discussed with the patient, was supported by genuine concern about treatment sensitivity, and later adverse effects on dopaminergic therapy show that those risks were real.

The proper question is therefore not whether clinicians had a reason to be cautious. They plainly did.

The unresolved question is whether the degree and duration of caution remained proportionate to the patient's functional burden as the case evolved.

**Relevant propositions:** PROP-0005, PROP-0006, PROP-0007

## 7. The record itself identifies the countervailing cost of deferral

The same early neurology record that justified delay acknowledged that the patient could in time require levodopa and that clinicians might later regret not starting it earlier.

In 2018 the neurologist recorded that the patient could be "very much better" if levodopa or another dopamine agonist were given, while still preferring not to do so.

By 2021 the patient felt undertreated and levodopa was finally introduced. By 2023 the treating team considered lack of levodopa a plausible explanation for substantial walking difficulty and OFF-related symptoms.

These entries do not prove that levodopa should have been commenced in 2017 or 2018. They do establish that the clinical balance had two sides throughout: future dyskinesia and behavioural risk on one side, present rigidity, gait impairment, pain and functional restriction on the other.

**Relevant propositions:** PROP-0006, PROP-0007, PROP-0010

## 8. Later dyskinesia both supports and complicates the original rationale

Subsequent chorea, dyskinesia and compulsive-behaviour concerns demonstrate genuine dopaminergic sensitivity. That evidence materially supports the legitimacy of caution.

But in 2022 the neurologist also recorded that delayed-start evidence indicated Sinemet may fuel or reveal dyskinesia but does not cause it, attributing the underlying susceptibility to the diseased brain's handling of dopamine.

That later formulation creates an expert issue. It may simply refine the earlier reasoning: avoiding or minimising levodopa exposure could still reduce the practical burden of dyskinesia even if it does not cause the underlying susceptibility.

Alternatively, it may mean that the protective value of prolonged levodopa deferral was overstated.

The documents cannot resolve that question without specialist evidence.

**Relevant propositions:** PROP-0006, PROP-0008

## 9. Genetic suspicion was not purely academic

In 2017 Prof Daniel Healy considered PARK2 sufficiently likely to recommend testing and linked that genotype with the possibility of prolonged management on relatively low doses of dopamine replacement together with dyskinesia if over-used.

The subsequent view that genetic testing could be delayed because it would not change management therefore creates a discrete expert question: whether genotype was in fact capable of informing the very treatment strategy that was being chosen.

The documentary record supports the existence of that tension. It does not establish that earlier genetic confirmation would necessarily have changed treatment or outcome.

**Relevant propositions:** PROP-0009

## 10. Pain should be analysed mechanistically, not categorically

The record does not support a simple binary choice between "Parkinson's pain" and "foot pain."

Potentially relevant mechanisms include local corn and callus pathology, clawing and altered foot mechanics, abnormal gait and loading, dystonia, OFF-state dystonia, central pain amplification and secondary musculoskeletal effects.

The repeated association between medication state and walking or foot relief is clinically important, but it does not by itself identify the generator.

The expert task is to determine which mechanisms best fit the chronology and whether a mixed neurological-biomechanical explanation is more plausible than a wholly separate structural disorder.

**Relevant propositions:** PROP-0011

## 11. What the documentary case establishes without expert opinion

The record itself establishes that:

- structural foot pathology was genuine and recurrent;
- Parkinsonism was established and affected gait, tone and movement;
- neurological treatment produced marked changes in function and later altered foot symptoms;
- levodopa was deliberately deferred for years because of anticipated treatment complications;
- genuine dyskinesia and behavioural concerns later occurred;
- the patient subsequently felt undertreated;
- later clinicians considered some walking difficulty, pain and lower-limb symptoms to be OFF-related or insufficiently treated;
- the relationship between the foot disorder and Parkinsonism remained disputed or incompletely resolved over time.

Those are documentary propositions. They do not require a negligence conclusion.

## 12. What requires expert opinion

Expert evidence is required to determine:

- whether the structural foot pathology was caused or materially aggravated by dystonia, rigidity or abnormal gait;
- the diagnostic significance of the 2017 treatment-response pattern;
- when, if ever, the original structural/neurological partition should have been reconsidered;
- whether the duration of levodopa deferral was reasonable;
- whether earlier levodopa would probably have produced meaningful functional benefit;
- how later dyskinesia should affect evaluation of the earlier treatment strategy;
- whether genetic testing could reasonably have altered management;
- the most likely mechanism or combination of mechanisms for the longstanding foot pain;
- whether and when the patient was clinically undertreated;
- and whether the overall longitudinal management remained coherent as the evidence changed.

## 13. Overall documentary position

The strongest case presented by the documents is not that one isolated clinical decision was necessarily wrong. It is that the record contains an accumulating sequence of evidence capable of destabilising two enduring assumptions: first, that the foot problem could be managed as substantially separate from the Parkinsonian disorder; and second, that prolonged dopaminergic restraint remained the appropriate balance throughout the patient's early course.

Whether those assumptions remained clinically defensible is the central matter for expert determination.

The evidential architecture should therefore remain:

**Clinical record -> Facts -> Tensions -> Propositions -> Expert Questions -> Clinical opinion**

The expert questions are the pivot. The ultimate argument should be built from their answers, not imposed in advance.
