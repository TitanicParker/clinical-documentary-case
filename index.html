Longitudinal clinical documentary analysis

An unresolved presentation, tested by what happened next.

This case examines the longitudinal relationship between structural foot pathology, dystonic and Parkinsonian lower-limb phenomena, dopaminergic treatment strategy, pain, treatment response and the eventual confirmation of Parkin-associated Parkinsonism and dystonia.

The case does not depend on proving that every foot symptom was neurological, that Podiatry treatment was inappropriate, or that levodopa necessarily should have been commenced in July 2017. Its central question is whether an initially unresolved clinical model remained adequate as the evidential position changed.

01

Governing issue

In 2017, severe young-onset Parkinsonism and genuine structural foot pathology were documented together. The neurological record did not establish a clean causal separation between the two. Instead, the relationship was expressly recorded as “Not clear.”

Parallel Neurology and Podiatry management may initially have been entirely reasonable. The question is what clinical significance should be attached to the evidence that accumulated afterwards.

At what point, if any, did accumulating neurological, pharmacological, treatment-state and genetic evidence require explicit reassessment of the original mixed foot/neurological presentation and/or the associated dopaminergic treatment strategy?

03

The clinical case

One evolving presentation

The pre-admission record progressively brought feet, gait, impaired movement initiation, increased tone, toe clawing, dexterity and functional impairment into one neurological referral pathway.

Two genuine documentary strands

Neurology documented severe Parkinsonism, dystonic-looking posture and a footdrop-like gait expressly described as “NOT FOOT DROP.” Podiatry separately documented genuine recurrent structural pathology, particularly at the left fifth metatarsophalangeal joint.

The relationship remained unresolved

The important documentary proposition is therefore not that one strand displaced the other. Both were real. What remained unresolved was how they interacted.

Core proposition Operational separation between Neurology and Podiatry did not, by itself, convert an expressly unresolved clinical relationship into a settled etiological separation.

Treatment-state evidence accumulated

Improvement on neurological treatment, deterioration during procyclidine withdrawal, later Parkinsonian interpretation of a foot-throw phenomenon, OFF-related findings, explicit undertreatment, and repeated pain improvement with dopaminergic medication progressively supplied evidence capable of bearing on the original partition.

At the same time, treatment restraint had a genuine rationale

Levodopa and dopamine-agonist treatment were deliberately restricted in a very young patient because of concern about dyskinesia, behavioural complications and long-term treatment burden. Later dyskinesia provides real support for the legitimacy of that caution.

The unresolved question is not whether caution was defensible in principle, but whether the balance between treatment risk and present symptom burden remained proportionate throughout the chronology.

04

Potential reassessment points

The case is best tested at identifiable decision points rather than by one retrospective allegation spanning the entire period.

2017

Express uncertainty

Parkinsonism, dystonic-looking gait and genuine structural foot disease coexist. Their relationship is recorded as “Not clear.”

2017–2018

Neurological treatment-state signal

Marked treatment response and withdrawal deterioration include lower-limb and outside-left-foot phenomena.

2018

Potential benefit acknowledged

The treating record states that the patient could be “very much better” on levodopa or a dopamine agonist while treatment remains deliberately deferred.

2020

Foot phenomenon classified as Parkinsonian

A foot-drop-like “throw” is interpreted as Parkinsonian in the presence of normal power.

2021

Levodopa introduced

Sinemet is commenced after the patient is recorded as feeling somewhat undertreated.

2023

Undertreatment becomes explicit

Consultant and Parkinson's nurse records contain direct OFF-related and undertreatment interpretations of gait, mobility and lower-limb symptoms.

2025

Sinemet CR produces major reported benefit

Severe morning foot disability leads to a cautious controlled-release levodopa trial. The subsequent record describes profound relief and effective analgesic benefit.

2026

Parkin aetiology confirmed

Genetic testing confirms two pathogenic Parkin variants and attributes both Parkinsonism and dystonia to compound heterozygous Parkin mutations.

05

Structural counterweight

The competing evidence is substantial.

Across the eight represented Podiatry treatment encounters between September 2017 and March 2019, pathology at or centred on the left fifth MTPJ was documented at every encounter. Toe deformity, callus formation and local mechanical treatment were genuine and recurrent.

Local treatment could help. Toe props were reported as very effective. Pregabalin later improved pain. Dopaminergic pain relief can also reflect modulation of the experience of pain rather than proof of its primary generator.

The evidence therefore does not support an absolute proposition that the feet were “neurological all along.”

Stronger formulation The clinically important issue is whether a clean structural-versus- neurological partition remained adequate once the longitudinal record increasingly supported interaction between structural pathology, dystonia, motor state, OFF periods and dopaminergic treatment.
06

Diagnostic trajectory

PRKN/PARK2 was identified as a serious etiological possibility in 2017 because of the unusually young onset and phenotype.

In March 2025, before genetic confirmation, the treating neurologist described the current belief and understanding as idiopathic Parkinson's disease.

In February 2026, specialist genetic testing confirmed two pathogenic Parkin variants and concluded that the patient's Parkinsonism and dystonia were due to compound heterozygous Parkin mutations.

What the genetic result does establish The etiological branch identified near the beginning of the case was ultimately confirmed, and dystonia was expressly included in the genetically defined disorder.
What it does not establish

It does not retrospectively prove the mechanism of every corn, callus, structural lesion or historical pain episode, and it does not by itself establish that the pre-confirmation idiopathic description was clinically unreasonable.

08

Expert hinge

Was the initial 2017 uncertainty and parallel-management strategy reasonably maintained throughout the subsequent chronology, or did the accumulating evidence create one or more identifiable dates at which a reasonable movement-disorders clinician should have reformulated the relationship between the neurological and foot/lower-limb presentation, altered the dopaminergic treatment strategy, obtained further specialist/genetic clarification, or some combination of those steps?

Until that question is independently answered, the documentary record supports expert investigation rather than a predetermined conclusion of clinical fault.
